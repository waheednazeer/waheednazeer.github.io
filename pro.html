An Ethical Dilemma:

One of my clients only has login credentials because they misplaced their wireless equipment's IP address. They needed to alter setups right away, but this problem prevented them from accessing their equipment. Because of their active network, we were unable to reset the equipment.

The incident was the fault of their site engineer and system administrator. Losing IP addresses is both careless and unprofessional. If the team cannot recover the IP addresses from the equipment, they may need to purchase additional hardware. Furthermore, we can describe the employees' failure to fulfill their responsibilities as unethical.

An alternative and explanation for the ethical dilemma:

An engineer is in charge of maintaining their network's functionality and safeguarding its vital data. The equipment model number, location, network address, login username, and password are among the crucial details. This is official network information that he is in charge of. He ought to let his management and other team members know about this.

This technical information about all network equipment should be recorded and kept in the network data center, and it should also be shared with the technical department.

According to Albert Bandura, agency is the ability of a person to consciously affect their own life and circumstances

by combining four fundamental characteristics: intentionality, self-reflectiveness, self-reactiveness, and planning.

My Reflection on Ethical Dilemma

I deal with a lot of ethical dilemmas in my line of work as a network engineer, mostly related to confidentiality and privacy, security obligations, intellectual property, and professional behavior. These conundrums frequently call for striking a balance between technical specifications and moral and legal duties to the public and individuals.

Negligence and improper handling of business data provided the initial ethical dilemma I examined in Week 2. In the event that I encounter a similar circumstance in the future, I will respond to it in accordance with business and industry norms.

  • I have access to enormous volumes of private and sensitive data that are moving across the network as a network engineer.
  • It is my ethical duty to ensure the confidentiality and integrity of this data and to prevent unauthorized access or disclosure.
  • If I become aware of any negligence or improper handling of corporate data, I will report it to the appropriate authorities within the organization.
  • I will also take steps to ensure that the data is handled in accordance with relevant laws and regulations, such as data protection and privacy laws.
  • I will also work to raise awareness among my colleagues and peers about the importance of data security and the ethical responsibilities that come with handling sensitive information.

My Guidelines for Handling Future Ethical Dilemmas:

  • I will always challenge justifications like "it's not my job" or "it's a known method to do things."
  • For most issues, have a calm, direct conversation with the person involved first.
  • Recognize the stakeholders, scenario, and opposing viewpoints.
  • I will report the problem to management if I am unable to fix it.
