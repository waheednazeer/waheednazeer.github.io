My Three Guiding Principles:

  • Integrity
  • Compassion
  • Purpose

Integrity, compassion, and purpose are my three guiding principles that have greatly influenced both my spiritual and professional experiences.
Strong connections, an inner compass, and significant growth in all facets of life are all facilitated by these guiding principles.

Integrity

Being honest, upholding moral norms, and continuously acting on high moral standards, even when no one is looking, are all components of integrity.

Verses on Integrity:

Alma 30:52-53: The Anti-Christ, Korihor, admits he has no power because he cannot persuade people to give up their integrity.

Doctrine and Covenants 121:45: "Let virtue garnish thy thoughts unceasingly; then shall thy confidence wax strong in the presence of God; and the Holy Ghost shall be thy constant companion, and thy confidence shall be an immovable monument to truth and honesty".

Proverbs 28:6: "Better is the poor man who walks in his integrity than one who is crooked in his ways, though he be rich".

Compassion

Compassion goes beyond just empathy; it involves that I truly understand others and act with kindness and a desire to help them, without controlling their path.

Verses on Compassion:

Doctrine and Covenants 121:1-3: While a prayer and not a direct command, it asks God to have His "bowels moved with compassion" toward His people.

Luke 6:36: "Therefore be merciful, just as your Father also is merciful".

Ephesians 4:32: "And be ye kind one to another, [and] tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you".

Purpose

What it means to have a purpose is to have a clear sense of purpose and meaning that inspires me to live honestly and contribute to society.

Verses on Purpose:

D&C 132:19: Teaches that those who receive eternal life will inherit all that God has, allowing them to enter God's rest.

2 Nephi 2:25: "Adam fell that men might be; and men are that they might have joy".

Jeremiah 29:11: "For I know the plans I have for you," declares the Lord, "plans to give you hope and a future".

Reflective Experience:

Unquestionably, going to a temple provides a deep sense of serenity, humility, and a connection to something bigger than oneself. It is a contemplative and transforming experience. I am convinced that these trips promote a fresh feeling of purpose and a strong sense of community belonging, as well as inner peace and clarity.

I am able to make better decisions and find a new sense of purpose or direction in life by using my quiet time for meditation and introspection in a spiritually charged setting. When I go to the temple, I also feel connected. Temples act as hubs for the community, uniting individuals from various walks of life who have similar beliefs or are looking for spiritual support and a sense of belonging. This promotes a sense of solidarity and unselfish service.

My Insights:

I believe that having agency greatly enhances professional skills by motivating ut to take initiative in acquiring, developing, and applying their abilities in the workplace. It encourages us to move away from merely reacting to job demands and instead take a proactive role in shaping our learning and work environment. Strong agency, in my opinion, enables professionals to match corporate aims with their own long-term objectives. This alignment improves overall performance and job satisfaction by boosting motivation, perseverance, and dedication to work duties.

To put it simply, agency transforms us from being merely recipients of training into self-directed, proactive, and continuously changing professionals by enhancing both our existing competencies and our ability to acquire new ones.

Week-5

Integrating Christlike Attributes

While identifying personal warning signs and creating realignment tactics demands reflection, self-awareness, and reliance on spiritual direction and support, I believe that integrating Christlike traits is a process of study, practice, and requesting God's assistance.

I paid money to an unfamiliar store owner for certain equipment recently, and the owner failed to deliver the promised item on time, which put me in a difficult predicament. My first response was one of annoyance and impatience; I started phoning him repeatedly and grumbling, concentrating only on my own timetable.

After giving this experience some thought, I saw that my egotism prevented me from exhibiting Christ's traits. I had the option to show greater empathy at that precise moment, maybe by saying something nice to the store owner or just waiting patiently, like the Savior would have.

Addressing Personal Warning Signs

My need to change from a "inward" to a "outward" emphasis was highlighted by this circumstance. I sensed the Spirit calling me to be mindful of the owner's possible stress and to react with kindness instead of annoyance.

I will explicitly ask the Lord to give me patience and opportunities to practice it in order to incorporate Christlike attributes, recognizing that patience is a gift from God that I developed with His assistance. Every time I encounter a circumstance that challenges my patience, I will deliberately stop, inhale deeply, and silently ask God for the fortitude to react with composure and generosity.

To address warning signs like personal impatience, I will focus on developing Christlike qualities. I will choose to practice patience, humility, and love by pausing to pray for God's perspective. I will observe the needs of those around me and respond with kindness and gentleness instead of anger. This approach will help me encourage growth in self-control and selfless service, as modeled by Jesus.
